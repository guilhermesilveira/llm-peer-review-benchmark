## Overall assessment

This is a clinically important, multicentre randomized trial that adds a controlled 24-week assessment of DNL343 in people with ALS after encouraging early-phase pharmacokinetic and biomarker findings. The research question is clear, and the randomized, placebo-controlled, intention-to-treat design is appropriate in principle. The primary result—DRR 1.04 (95% CrI, 0.83–1.32; posterior probability DRR <1, 0.37)—provides no evidence of benefit. The trial is therefore relevant to clinicians and patients, while its findings should be interpreted as a lack of demonstrated efficacy rather than proof that no effect is possible.

The principal limitations of the report are that key features of the Bayesian primary model and the shared-control analysis are not sufficiently transparent; differential loss to follow-up is not adequately addressed; and the pharmacodynamic and safety findings need fuller presentation. These issues materially affect the interpretation of this negative trial and should be addressed before publication. Ethical oversight and consent are described. The supplied review copy omits the references and citation markers, so I cannot assess the currency or completeness of the bibliography.

## Major comments

1. **Please provide sufficient detail to evaluate the primary Bayesian model and its result.**  
   The primary outcome is central to the paper, but the description of the “bayesian shared parameter model” is not sufficient for readers to understand or reproduce the analysis. Please report the prior distributions, model specification and link between the ALSFRS-R and survival components, any random-effects structure, and the assumptions used for residual variation and missing observations. State whether the model and priors were prespecified and whether there were deviations from the statistical analysis plan. Please also report relevant convergence and model-diagnostic information, and give the results of the prior-sensitivity analyses.

   The reported DRR of 1.04 is compatible with some slowing as well as with worsening. Since the sample-size calculation was based on detecting a 30% slowing, please report the posterior probability of the prespecified 30% effect (corresponding to a DRR of 0.70), or otherwise explain what clinically meaningful benefit the posterior distribution can and cannot exclude. The sensitivity analysis changing the common treatment-effect assumption is summarized only as a range; please give enough detail to interpret it. Finally, report the number of deaths and the treatment contrast for mortality. The model’s mortality hazard is reported as 0.007 per month in each group, but readers need to know how much this sparse component contributed to the integrated primary estimate. Please also reconcile the primary model’s use of “survival” with Figure 2B’s description of death or permanent assisted ventilation.

2. **Clarify the shared-placebo design and the assumptions underlying its use.**  
   The primary efficacy population combines 63 placebo recipients randomized within the DNL343 regimen with 76 placebo recipients from another concurrently enrolling regimen. This is an important feature of the analysis, but the report does not adequately describe the contributing regimen or establish the comparability of its participants, eligibility criteria, follow-up, background care, outcome ascertainment, and calendar period with those in the DNL343 regimen. Please explain how these controls were selected for pooling and how the model “accommodates potential differences in the shared control across regimens.”

   The regimen-only sensitivity analysis is relevant: the primary DRR changes from 1.04 in the ECC population to 1.19 in the ERO population, and the placebo-group ALSFRS-R slope also changes. Please explain this shift and discuss the extent to which the main estimate depends on the shared controls. Also make clear wherever results are presented that the regimen-specific placebo group (n=63) is included within the shared-placebo group (n=139), rather than being an independent comparison group. This is especially important in Table 1 and in the safety results.

3. **Address missing outcome data and differential follow-up.**  
   Follow-up was discontinued before week 24 by 30 of 186 participants in the DNL343 group (16.1%) and 13 of 139 in the shared-placebo group (9.4%). The report states that all available data were analyzed, but does not adequately explain the assumptions made about outcomes after withdrawal or how potentially informative nonfatal dropout was assessed. The joint model addresses loss of ALSFRS-R data due to mortality, but it is not clear how it addresses withdrawal for other reasons.

   Please provide a participant flow and reasons for discontinuation by group and over time, clarify which participants continued outcome assessments after stopping study drug, and state how missing data were handled in each analysis. Please add sensitivity analyses for potentially informative dropout if available, or acknowledge more directly how it limits interpretation. Report treatment adherence and dose interruption as well as treatment discontinuation, so readers can assess drug exposure in the efficacy population.

4. **Provide quantitative and interpretable pharmacodynamic results.**  
   The discussion correctly notes that this trial did not confirm pathway modulation, but the pharmacodynamic results are too sparsely reported to assess that conclusion. Only 44 DNL343-treated participants and 16 placebo participants are reported for CSF GDF-15, yet no numerical between-group estimate, uncertainty interval, or exact analysis method is provided. Please report the baseline and week-24 values, the between-group change estimate with an interval, the number with paired samples, and reasons for missing samples. The Methods mention optional CSF sampling at week 24, whereas the Results describe change from baseline; please explain how and when baseline CSF samples were obtained.

   The Discussion says that ISR biomarker data were available in “approximately 25%” of participants, but this denominator is unclear and appears not to describe the CSF subset overall. Please clarify which biomarkers were measured, in how many participants per group, and what proportion of the randomized population these represent. Drug exposure and CSF penetration are not evidence of target engagement in this trial; the abstract and discussion should distinguish those findings from the lack of a demonstrated pharmacodynamic effect here.

5. **Complete the reporting of prespecified outcomes and clarify the secondary-outcome inference.**  
   The prespecified hierarchy includes “HHD upper and grip strength,” but Table 2 reports only upper-extremity HHD. Please provide the grip-strength result and the results for all other prespecified secondary outcomes, including relevant event counts. If any results are only in the supplement, make this clear in the text.

   The abstract says that secondary outcomes “were not statistically different,” while Table 2 shows a nominal difference in log-transformed NfL (difference 0.081; 95% CI, 0.009–0.153; P=.03). The Results and Discussion explain that this was not significant under the prespecified hierarchy, but the abstract should do so as well: no secondary outcome met the confirmatory testing procedure because the primary endpoint was not met, while NfL showed a nominal, multiplicity-unadjusted difference. Please avoid language that could be read as establishing either benefit or harm from that isolated result.

6. **Present the safety findings with appropriate qualification and clarify overlapping groups.**  
   The abstract describes adverse-event incidence rates as similar. However, serious adverse events were numerically more frequent with DNL343 than with shared placebo (15.1% vs 11.5%), and discontinuation due to adverse events was also more frequent (9.7% vs 5.8%). Three serious adverse events were attributed to DNL343 by investigators. These numbers do not establish a safety difference, but they warrant a more qualified summary than “similar.” Please give fuller detail on the serious and drug-attributed events, their outcomes, and the laboratory, ECG, and other safety assessments, and ensure the abstract reflects the observed numerical differences.

   In addition, the regimen-specific placebo participants are a subset of the shared-placebo group. The presentation of fatal adverse events and serious adverse events for both groups could be misread as reporting independent groups or additive counts. Please state explicitly that these categories overlap and avoid double counting.

7. **Clarify trial conduct, blinding, and sponsor involvement.**  
   The report states that the study was double blind and that participants were randomized within medication strata, but does not describe sequence generation, allocation concealment, who was blinded, or how the regimen and treatment assignments were implemented. Please provide these details or point clearly to the relevant protocol sections. The sponsor’s role is described only as collaboration in trial design and conduct. Please specify the sponsor’s role in data collection, analysis, interpretation, manuscript preparation, and the decision to submit, and identify who had access to the unblinded data. Also report whether any interim analysis led to a decision or adaptation for this regimen and how the prespecified operating characteristics account for the platform design and shared controls.

8. **Temper the claim about the active-treatment extension.**  
   The Discussion says exploratory ATE data “revealed no longer-term effects” and suggests that a longer placebo-controlled period would not have changed the results. No ATE results are presented here, and an uncontrolled extension cannot establish what the longer-term placebo-controlled contrast would have been. Please provide the relevant data and explain their limitations, or remove or substantially qualify this inference. The main conclusion should remain focused on the 24-week randomized comparison.

## Minor comments

1. The conclusion that “DNL343 did not slow disease progression” is stronger than the data warrant. Prefer wording such as “DNL343 did not demonstrate evidence of slowing disease progression over 24 weeks,” consistent with the abstract’s more cautious phrasing.
2. The study population was recruited in the US and was 92.6% White, with relatively few Black or Asian participants. Please note this limitation when discussing generalizability, along with the eligibility restrictions of disease duration of 36 months or less and vital capacity of at least 50%.
3. Please clarify how the planned sample size of 240 relates to the 249 participants randomized within the DNL343 regimen and the 76 additional shared-placebo participants.
4. The abstract should make clear that the primary result is a Bayesian estimate and retain the credible-interval terminology used in the Results. The wording “incidence rates” for adverse events should also be checked if the reported figures are proportions of participants with events.

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** The trial addresses an important question and reports a potentially valuable negative result, but fuller reporting of the primary analysis, shared-control design, missing data, pharmacodynamic findings, and safety is needed for readers to judge its reliability and clinical meaning.
