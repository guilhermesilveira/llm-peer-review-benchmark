## Overall assessment

This is an important negative randomized trial of a mechanistically motivated treatment for ALS. On the account presented, its main contribution is a randomized evaluation of eIF2B activation beyond the safety, pharmacokinetic, and biomarker findings of earlier studies. The results are relevant to clinicians and patients, as well as to investigators developing ALS therapies and using shared-control platform designs.

The overall design is appropriate, and the consistency of the primary and regimen-specific analyses supports the conclusion that efficacy was not demonstrated. Ethical approval, informed consent, and independent safety monitoring are appropriately described. However, several issues require clarification before the reliability and implications of the findings can be fully assessed: exchangeability of shared controls, differential loss to follow-up, the definition and ascertainment of survival outcomes, and important details of the primary model. Safety and mechanistic conclusions also need more cautious presentation.

**Recommendation: major revision.**

## Major points

### 1. Establish the validity of the shared-control comparison

The manuscript does not provide enough information to judge whether the 76 placebo participants from the contributing regimen are appropriate controls for DNL343.

Please describe:

- How the initial regimen assignment was generated and concealed, including which regimens were available to each participant.
- How concurrency was defined, including calendar overlap and availability of the regimens at participating sites.
- Whether contributing placebo participants satisfied the DNL343-specific eligibility criteria and could have been assigned to DNL343.
- Relevant differences between regimens in eligibility, visit schedules, assessments, background treatment, and placebo administration.
- Precisely how the primary model accommodates differences between placebo groups across regimens.

Table 1 should include the contributing-regimen placebo group separately, or provide its characteristics in a supplementary table. The regimen-specific placebo group is already a subset of shared placebo; this nesting should be made particularly clear.

There are notable baseline imbalances: women comprise 44.1% of the DNL343 group, 33.8% of shared placebo, and 20.6% of regimen-specific placebo. Baseline NfL is also higher in the active group. These differences do not establish a failure of randomization, but their potential implications should be examined. The change from a DRR of 1.04 using shared controls to 1.19 using regimen-specific controls makes understanding the control-group differences particularly important. Explain whether sensitivity analyses addressing important prognostic imbalances not already represented in the adjustment variables materially alter the results.

Finally, repeated master-protocol screening is described. Please clarify whether participants could enter this regimen after previous platform treatment, how prior treatment and washout were handled, and how duplicate participation was excluded from the analysis.

### 2. Differential attrition is a substantial unresolved threat to inference

Before week 24, follow-up was discontinued for 30 of 186 DNL343 participants (16.1%) versus 13 of 139 shared-placebo participants (9.4%). Nonfatal discontinuations were also more frequent with DNL343. Including all randomized participants “with all available data” is appropriate, but does not by itself eliminate bias from missing outcomes.

Please provide:

- Reasons and timing of withdrawal by group, separating treatment discontinuation from withdrawal of outcome follow-up.
- The reason for the sponsor-initiated withdrawal.
- Numbers with observed ALSFRS-R, SVC, HHD, and NfL measurements at each assessment.
- Completeness of vital-status and ventilation ascertainment after treatment discontinuation or withdrawal.
- The assumptions about missing outcomes used by each analysis.

The joint model addresses death under its specified assumptions; it does not automatically address informative nonfatal withdrawal. Sensitivity analyses should investigate plausible departures from missing-at-random assumptions, for example through a pattern-mixture or tipping-point analysis. Please distinguish these from sensitivity analyses concerned only with death.

The intended estimand should also be stated explicitly, including how treatment discontinuation, changes in background therapy, and entry into the extension are handled.

### 3. Clarify the survival endpoint, analysis window, and event counts

The terminology is inconsistent. The primary outcome is described as function and “survival,” the results report a “mortality hazard rate,” and Figure 2B concerns death or permanent assisted ventilation. The methods also refer to death-equivalent events “including tracheostomy.”

Please specify exactly which events enter:

1. The survival component of the primary model;
2. The hierarchical secondary survival endpoint;
3. The supportive joint-rank analysis.

Define the treatment of tracheostomy and describe event ascertainment and any adjudication.

Report deaths and death-equivalent events separately by treatment group, with numbers at risk and censoring information. The participant-flow text lists three DNL343 deaths before week 24, whereas the safety section reports four fatal TEAEs. This may reflect different observation windows or classification after withdrawal, but it requires explicit reconciliation.

The analysis-window description is difficult to follow, particularly the references to week 24, the last participant’s opportunity to complete follow-up, the final safety visit, and extension entry. State the precise observation window for each efficacy and safety analysis, including whether any survival or safety follow-up extends beyond week 24. Placebo-controlled outcomes must be clearly distinguished from outcomes after active-treatment extension entry.

### 4. Provide sufficient detail to evaluate the primary Bayesian analysis

The primary model is central to the paper but is described largely conceptually. The main article should give an accessible account, with full specifications in the protocol or statistical appendix, of:

- The functional and survival submodels and the mathematical relationship of the DRR to each.
- Priors, including those governing treatment effects and borrowing across placebo groups.
- The participant-level relationship between functional trajectory and survival, and the assumptions under which the model addresses informative truncation of functional measurements by death.
- How within-person correlation and regimen differences are modeled.
- Computational convergence and relevant model checks.

There appear to be few survival events. Consequently, the survival component may be weakly informed by the data and sensitive to modeling assumptions. Please identify and report the analyses that relax the common-treatment-effect assumption and the exponential survival assumption. Providing only a range of sensitivity-analysis DRRs does not show which assumptions matter.

The sample-size description should identify the assumed number of shared controls, expected progression and event rates, and allowance for missing data. State how many interim analyses were actually conducted, whether they led to any action, and how the reported operating characteristics incorporated the planned monitoring.

### 5. Present safety more fully and avoid implying equivalence

The abstract’s statement that adverse-event incidence was “similar” is too reassuring without qualification. Compared with shared placebo, DNL343 had numerically higher proportions of:

- Any TEAE: 93.0% versus 87.8%;
- Serious adverse events: 15.1% versus 11.5%;
- Drug discontinuation because of adverse events: 9.7% versus 5.8%.

Three serious adverse events were attributed to DNL343: elevated transaminases, electrocardiographic ST-segment abnormalities, and drug eruption. These findings do not establish an excess risk, but neither does this trial establish comparable safety.

Please include a compact safety table in the main article, retaining regimen-specific placebo comparisons. Provide comparator frequencies for fatigue, headache, and dizziness, rather than reporting only active-group counts. For the three drug-attributed serious events, give clinically useful details on severity, timing, management, resolution, and any rechallenge. Summarize relevant laboratory, ECG, suicidality, and vital-sign findings.

Report treatment exposure, adherence, and dose interruptions. Given differential withdrawal, explain whether exposure-adjusted summaries are informative. Risk differences with confidence intervals for serious adverse events and adverse-event-related discontinuation would help convey uncertainty; formal significance testing is not necessary.

Use “proportions” rather than “incidence rates” where the denominator is participants rather than person-time. Fatal events should be described as not considered treatment-related by investigators, rather than as definitively not attributable to treatment.

### 6. Separate CNS exposure, target engagement, and dose adequacy

The pharmacokinetic findings support CNS exposure, but do not establish target engagement or an optimal dose. The abstract’s assertion of “optimal dose selection” is particularly difficult to sustain when the current trial did not demonstrate the expected GDF-15 response.

The CSF analysis also needs more complete reporting:

- Methods describe optional CSF collection at week 24, but results report change from baseline. When and how were baseline samples collected?
- Do the 44 active and 16 placebo participants represent complete paired samples?
- Were the placebo samples regimen-specific or shared?
- What were the baseline values, group changes, between-group estimate, and confidence interval?
- How did sampled participants differ from those without paired samples?
- What were the assay modifications, precision, and masking procedures?

The statement that biomarker data were available in “approximately 25%” of participants needs a specified denominator: 60 participants are approximately 18.5% of the ECC population, although approximately 24% of the DNL343-regimen population.

The discussion appropriately acknowledges uncertainty in measuring ISR modulation. Maintain that distinction consistently: absent GDF-15 change does not necessarily prove absent target engagement, but prior-phase biomarker findings and current CSF drug concentrations do not prove adequate pathway modulation in this trial. State whether other prespecified ISR biomarker analyses were performed and report them, or explain their absence.

### 7. Report all key prespecified efficacy analyses with estimates, not qualitative conclusions alone

The supportive joint-rank analysis is summarized only as showing “no evidence of benefit.” Provide the numerical result, its uncertainty where applicable, and the relevant test result.

The hierarchy includes “HHD upper and grip strength,” but Table 2 reports only upper-extremity HHD. Clarify whether grip strength was a separate prespecified endpoint and report it accordingly. Ensure the hierarchy and all listed endpoint definitions match the protocol and analysis plan.

All secondary P values in Table 2 should be explicitly labeled nominal because the primary endpoint did not meet the success threshold. The distinction between lack of confirmatory significance and absence of any observed difference is particularly important for NfL, for which the ECC comparison was nominally unfavorable.

For interpretation, the DRR interval is compatible, under the specified model, with approximately 17% slower to 32% faster progression. Thus, the data do not support the targeted 30% slowing, but they do not establish exact absence of effect or exclude worsening. This uncertainty would be more useful to clinical readers than significance statements alone.

### 8. Remove or substantially qualify the inference from the extension, and align the conclusions throughout

The manuscript explicitly reports the randomized portion, yet the discussion introduces unshown extension results and concludes that longer placebo-controlled treatment probably would not have changed the findings:

> “exploratory data from the ATE period … revealed no longer-term effects … suggesting that longer placebo-controlled treatment duration would not have changed the results.”

This inference is not supported by the material presented. Either remove the claim or present the extension methods and results, including participant selection, follow-up, attrition, and analytical limitations. Even then, an extension in which all participants receive active treatment cannot establish what a longer randomized placebo-controlled comparison would have shown.

The abstract and conclusions should consistently state that **200 mg daily did not demonstrate slowing over the studied randomized period in the enrolled population**. They should not imply that optimal dosing has been established, that the ISR pathway has been invalidated, or that alternative pathways necessarily must replace this approach. The discussion itself makes the more defensible point that this trial does not justify abandoning ISR targeting.

The abstract should also acknowledge the nominal NfL increase and summarize safety with greater balance, rather than allowing “no secondary differences” and “similar adverse events” to obscure potentially relevant findings.

## Minor points

1. **Outcome measurement:** Briefly describe which assessments were performed by telephone versus in person, and how consistency was maintained across sites. Define the HHD muscle set, aggregation, and normalization. Explain how inability to perform SVC or strength testing because of disease progression was handled.

2. **Table 2 units:** Distinguish percentage-point changes in percent-predicted SVC from relative percentage changes. State the logarithm base for NfL. Clarify that the back-transformed NfL treatment contrast is a relative geometric-mean contrast, not simply the difference between the two displayed percentage changes.

3. **Baseline measures:** Explain the positive sign convention for the “prebaseline ALSFRS-R slope,” and briefly describe how the TRICALS score is constructed or direct readers clearly to its specification.

4. **Generalizability:** Discuss the predominantly White population, relatively limited bulbar-onset representation, and eligibility restrictions concerning disease duration, respiratory function, and swallowing. “Relatively broad population” should not obscure these restrictions.

5. **Timeline:** Distinguish recruitment, randomized follow-up, extension follow-up, and database-lock dates. This would make the May 2023–May 2025 study dates and the “20 months” to top-line results easier to reconcile.

6. **Transparency:** Clarify responsibility for the analyses, academic investigators’ access to the data, and the sponsor’s role in interpretation, manuscript preparation, and publication decisions. Ensure funding, competing-interest, data-sharing, registration, and protocol-amendment information are complete in the final submission.

7. The reference list, figures, and supplementary materials were not included in this review copy. I therefore cannot assess reference currency or completeness, inspect the figures, or verify whether the technical specifications requested above are already fully documented in the supplements.

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** This trial could make a worthwhile contribution, and the reported results consistently fail to demonstrate efficacy. Publication should follow clarification of shared-control validity, differential attrition, survival definitions and observation windows, and primary-model assumptions, together with more balanced safety reporting and narrower mechanistic and extension-related conclusions.
