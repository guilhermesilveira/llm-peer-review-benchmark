This manuscript addresses an important and potentially original question: whether extending adjuvant icotinib to 3 years improves outcomes after resection and chemotherapy in EGFR-mutated lung adenocarcinoma. The long follow-up is a clear strength, and a DFS benefit in this selected population could matter to clinicians and patients. However, the current version has several major problems that limit confidence in the findings: the allocation procedure is not clearly random, the primary efficacy analysis is per-protocol rather than intention-to-treat, there are multiple internal numerical inconsistencies, and the conclusions greatly overreach the data.

Major points

1. Randomization/allocation concealment are not adequately described and, as written, appear not to be random.
   - The Methods state that “the site investigator assigned patients alternately to icotinib or placebo in the order in which they were enrolled, separately within each stratum (the first patient in a stratum to icotinib, the next to placebo, and so on).”
   - This is predictable alternation, not a clearly random sequence with allocation concealment. If this is the actual method used, it is a major threat to internal validity and selection bias.
   - Please clarify exactly how the sequence was generated, concealed, and implemented. If “alternately” is a shorthand for a concealed block-randomization scheme, that should be stated explicitly. If alternation was truly used, I do not think the trial can be considered adequately randomized.

2. The primary efficacy analysis should not rely only on the per-protocol set.
   - The manuscript states that DFS and OS were analyzed in the per-protocol set, not the full randomized population.
   - Nineteen randomized patients were excluded from the efficacy analysis (141 randomized vs 122 in the PPS), and follow-up was incomplete for a substantial proportion of the PPS (“the remaining 41 patients stopped attending scheduled imaging assessments before any recurrence was documented”).
   - This raises concern about informative censoring and bias. In a randomized phase 3 trial, an intention-to-treat analysis should be presented as the primary or at least a key sensitivity analysis, with clear reasons for exclusion from the PPS by arm.
   - Please also explain why 141 patients were randomized despite a planned sample size of 124, and whether the power calculation was updated after the protocol amendment.

3. The manuscript contains several internal inconsistencies that must be corrected.
   - The Results section says “147 patients were enrolled and randomly assigned,” but the treatment groups total 141 (68 + 73). This arithmetic error must be corrected.
   - The abstract reports the primary DFS result as HR 0.40 (95% CI 0.24–0.66; p < 0.001), whereas the Results section reports HR 0.50 (95% CI 0.30–0.82; p = 0.0061) for the same endpoint and population. These values cannot both be correct and need reconciliation.
   - The Results section states: “The 3-year DFS rate was lower in the icotinib group than in the placebo group (58.5% ... vs. 36.1% ...), as was the 5-year DFS rate...” This sentence is reversed; the numbers show the opposite.
   - In the safety text, placebo TEAEs are reported as 22/96 rather than 22/69.
   - These errors undermine confidence in the data presentation and must be fully checked against the tables, figures, and statistical output.

4. The conclusions are far too broad and are not supported by the data.
   - The trial did not show an OS benefit (HR 0.84, 95% CI 0.39–1.81; p = 0.65).
   - The trial enrolled only Chinese patients with stage II–IIIA lung adenocarcinoma who had completed four cycles of platinum-based chemotherapy. It does not address stage IB disease, non-Asian populations, or patients who did not receive chemotherapy.
   - The manuscript’s statements that icotinib “should replace osimertinib” and that this applies “worldwide, including those with stage IB disease, non-Asian patients, and patients who have not received adjuvant chemotherapy” are not justified by the study.
   - Cross-trial comparisons with ADAURA and other studies should be presented much more cautiously. Without a head-to-head comparison, this manuscript cannot support claims of superiority over osimertinib.
   - The abstract and conclusion should be rewritten to say that 3-year adjuvant icotinib improved DFS in the studied population, not that it prolongs survival or should replace osimertinib globally.

5. The subgroup and biomarker analyses are exploratory and need much clearer handling.
   - The subgroup findings are presented as “consistent,” but stage II disease (HR 0.57; p = 0.10) and stage IIIA disease (HR 0.62; p = 0.071) were not statistically significant. Please provide treatment-by-subgroup interaction tests rather than relying on within-subgroup p values.
   - The ctDNA/MRD analysis is especially underdeveloped for a biomarker-guided claim. Please provide the assay details, definition of MRD positivity, numbers by arm, and interaction testing. The reported numbers (75 ctDNA-MRD-positive and 30 negative) do not clearly reconcile with the 102 patients with baseline tissue profiling.
   - These analyses should be clearly labeled hypothesis-generating, not practice-changing.
   - The quality-of-life instrument was developed by the investigators for this trial; please provide evidence of validation and explain the basis for the ≥6-point decline threshold.

Minor points

1. Please clarify how DFS follow-up was calculated (eg, reverse Kaplan-Meier or another method), because the reported follow-up differs between groups and the censoring pattern is substantial.

2. The manuscript uses “clinical stage” in Table 1, but the Methods describe pathologically confirmed stage II–IIIA disease. Please clarify whether the baseline stage is pathological or clinical and align the terminology throughout.

3. Table 1 reports completion of 4-cycle chemotherapy as 63/66 and 67/69, which is unexpected because completion was required for eligibility. Please explain the missing data and how many patients truly completed the required chemotherapy before randomization.

4. Please clarify the denominator for subsequent therapies. The statement that “osimertinib [was] the most commonly used agent in the icotinib group (39.4% [26/66]) and icotinib in the placebo group (44.9% [31/69])” is hard to interpret without giving the number who recurred and the number who actually received postrecurrence treatment.

5. Please report whether any interstitial lung disease occurred, since this was specifically highlighted in the Methods as a safety concern.

6. The reference list is omitted in the review copy, so I cannot assess completeness. Please ensure the final manuscript includes balanced, up-to-date citation of the key adjuvant EGFR-TKI trials and does not selectively emphasize studies that support the preferred conclusion.

7. The manuscript needs thorough copy editing for numerical and wording errors, especially in the abstract and Results.

Recommendation: major revision
