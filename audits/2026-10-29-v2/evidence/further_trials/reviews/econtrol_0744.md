This is an important and generally well-conducted randomized trial. The manuscript is clear, the clinical question is highly relevant, and the negative efficacy result is itself important for ALS research and for readers interested in disease-modifying therapy development. The platform-trial design and the pharmacokinetic work are strengths. However, I think several issues need to be addressed before publication, mainly around statistical transparency, consistency of safety reporting, and the interpretation of the open-label extension data.

**Major points**

1. **The discussion over-interprets the open-label extension data.**  
   In the Discussion, the authors state: *“exploratory data from the ATE period of the study revealed no longer-term effects on neurofilament light chain levels and clinical outcomes, suggesting that longer placebo-controlled treatment duration would not have changed the results.”* This is not warranted from the data presented. The ATE is open-label and uncontrolled, so it is vulnerable to survivor bias, selection bias, and confounding by time. If the authors wish to mention ATE findings, they should be presented explicitly as exploratory and hypothesis-generating only, and the conclusion about placebo-controlled duration should be removed or substantially softened.

2. **The safety reporting needs to be reconciled more clearly.**  
   The Results say: *“Before week 24, follow-up was discontinued by 30 participants in the DNL343 group (26 terminated participation early, 3 died, and 1 was withdrawn by the sponsor), 8 in the regimen-specific placebo group (6 terminated participation early and 2 died), and 13 in the shared placebo group overall (10 terminated participation early and 3 died)”* yet later *“Fatal TEAEs occurred in 4 participants … in the DNL343 group, 3 … in the shared placebo group, and 2 … in the regimen-specific placebo group.”* These numbers do not line up unless one fatal event occurred after the week-24 placebo-controlled window. Please clarify the safety reporting window, reconcile the death counts, and state explicitly whether the fatal TEAEs include events during the active treatment extension or other post-week-24 follow-up.

3. **The primary Bayesian/shared-control analysis needs fuller statistical reporting and a clearer justification of the pooled placebo strategy.**  
   The primary inference rests on *“a bayesian shared parameter model of function and survival”* and on sharing placebo participants from another concurrent regimen. Because this is a nonstandard analysis and the manuscript’s main conclusion depends on it, please provide:
   - the prior distributions used;
   - basic model diagnostics/convergence information;
   - a clearer explanation of how exchangeability of the shared placebo groups was assessed/assumed;
   - and, ideally, sensitivity analyses with additional prognostic adjustment.  
   This is especially relevant because the baseline characteristics are not identical across groups: for example, in Table 1 the DNL343 group is 55.9% male, the shared placebo group 66.2% male, and baseline serum NfL also differs somewhat. I note that the regimen-specific placebo sensitivity analysis gives a median DRR of 1.19 (95% CrI 0.92-1.58), so the result is not obviously driven in the favorable direction by pooling, but the manuscript should explain why the pooled control is still appropriate.  
   Relatedly, the analysis-population definition is difficult to follow, particularly the phrase *“excluding any placebo participant from the contributing regimen who was also randomized within the DNL343 regimen.”* Please clarify whether any individual contributed to more than one regimen and how repeated screening/randomization across the master protocol was handled.

4. **The biomarker analysis is underpowered and should be framed more cautiously.**  
   CSF GDF-15 was measured in only a subset of participants (*“44 receiving DNL343 and 16 receiving placebo”*). Because sampling was optional and the subset is small and imbalanced, the absence of a biomarker effect cannot be taken as strong evidence against target engagement. Please provide more detail on who contributed CSF samples, whether that subset was representative, and ensure the biomarker findings are clearly presented as exploratory.

**Minor points**

1. **Abstract wording on secondary endpoints should be tightened.**  
   The abstract says *“Secondary outcome measures were not statistically different”* even though Table 2 shows a nominal NfL signal in the ECC analysis (P = .03) that was not robust to the regimen-specific placebo sensitivity analysis. It would be more accurate to say that no secondary endpoint met the prespecified hierarchical threshold, with the NfL finding described as nominal/exploratory.

2. **Please define the log transformation more clearly.**  
   Table 2 reports *“Serum NfL level, log(pg/mL)”* but does not specify whether this is natural log or log10. That should be stated explicitly.

3. **Clarify the direction of the prebaseline ALSFRS-R slope.**  
   In Table 1, *“Prebaseline ALSFRS-R slope, mean (SD), points/mo”* is reported as positive values. Since ALSFRS-R is a decline score in ALS, it would help readers if the sign convention were stated clearly.

4. **The safety summary is a little too reassuring.**  
   The manuscript states that the *“incidence rates of adverse events were similar”* between groups, but serious adverse events and discontinuations due to adverse events were numerically higher with DNL343. This should be described more precisely.

5. **Generalizability should be acknowledged.**  
   The study population was predominantly White (Table 1: 92.6% White in the active/shared-placebo population), with relatively few Black participants and a small Hispanic/Latino minority. Please acknowledge the limited racial and ethnic diversity and the implications for generalizability.

6. **References.**  
   The reference list was omitted from the copy I received, so I cannot assess completeness or currency.

**Recommendation: major revision.**
